**Korea Economy, Checked** tests Korean economic policies and market claims against published data. Every report follows the same structure, so readers can compare reports and see exactly what each conclusion rests on.

## What every report contains

- **A verdict** on the policy or claim, using the scale below
- **Key numbers**, each with a data-status label and a link to its source
- **Counter-evidence** — data that cuts against our verdict is reported, not left out
- **Who bears the cost** — which region, income group or type of household is affected
- **Sources**, listed at the end with the date we accessed them

## Verdict scale

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Effective | The data shows the policy reaching its stated goal |
| Partially effective | Some goals are met, others are not, or effects are uneven across groups |
| Too early to judge | Not enough time or data has passed to assess the policy |
| Not effective | The data does not show progress toward the stated goal |
| Risk of backfiring | The data points to effects opposite to the stated goal |

We judge every government, party and institution by the same standard. A verdict is about a policy's results, not about who proposed it.

## Data-status labels

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| verified | We checked the figure against the original publisher (a ministry, the Bank of Korea, Statistics Korea, the Korea Real Estate Board, and similar) |
| reference | The figure was reported by a news outlet or a secondary source, and we could not confirm it at the original publisher |
| estimate | We calculated the figure ourselves; the method is explained in the text |

Only verified figures are used as the basis for a verdict. Reference figures and estimates are shown as context and are labeled as such.

## Korean terms and currency

- Korean-specific terms (for example *jeonse*, the lump-sum deposit lease) are explained the first time they appear in a report.
- Won amounts are shown first, with a US dollar conversion and the exchange-rate date where it helps.
- Many reports are adapted from our Korean-language research. When they are, the Korean original is linked at the end.

<h2 id="corrections">Corrections</h2>

If a figure, date or source in a report is wrong, email **ceo@stargateedu.co.kr** with the report title and the correction. When we correct a report, we update its date and add a note at the end describing what changed. We do not remove corrections silently.
