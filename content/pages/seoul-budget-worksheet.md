A budget is useful when everybody knows what each number covers. A room price is usually not the same kind of number as a meal allowance. Separate shared accommodation, per-person daily spending and whole-trip extras before adding them together.

## Use the same units throughout

The **[free planner's preparation and budget desk](/#planning-desk)** uses Korean won and these rules:

| Input | What to enter | How it is counted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| People | Everyone covered by this estimate | Used for food, transport and the per-person total |
| Accommodation nights | Nights you will actually pay for | Separate from sightseeing days |
| All rooms per night | The combined nightly amount for every room | Multiplied by nights, once for the group |
| Food per person per day | Your own daily allowance for one person | Multiplied by people and sightseeing days |
| Local transport per person per day | Your own local transport allowance | Multiplied by people and sightseeing days |
| Activities for the stay | The complete amount for the whole group | Added once |
| Other / contingency | Other costs for the whole group and stay | Added once |

The calculator uses **your inputs**. It does not fetch hotel prices, taxi fares, meal prices or ticket availability. Changing from three sightseeing days to five changes the daily-cost calculation; review accommodation nights separately if your stay differs.

## Check the costs that are easy to leave out

Flights, travel insurance, shopping, airport transport, extra nights and fees belong in your overall plan when they apply. Put them into your own “Other” allowance or track them separately. A blank field is an amount you have not included, not a promise that something is free.

For friends, agree on which expenses are shared and how you will settle differences. For families, compare the actual room arrangement and activity terms for your group. For solo travelers, remember that a shared room cost may become a one-person cost.
