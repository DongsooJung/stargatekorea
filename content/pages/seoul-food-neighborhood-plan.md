The most useful food itinerary starts with a neighborhood, not ten restaurants on opposite sides of Seoul. Choose one market or meal anchor, add a nearby cafe or walk, and leave enough space to change your mind.

## A market day with breathing room

Gwangjang Market can be the anchor of a central Seoul food day. Look at the menu, portion size and complete price before ordering. Choose a few things you want to try rather than treating every stall as a required stop.

Cheonggyecheon or an evening neighborhood visit can be optional additions. Check the walking or transit connection from your actual starting point. In wet weather, a longer indoor meal or cafe stop may be a better use of your time.

## A different day in Mapo

Consider Mangwon Market and the surrounding neighborhood for a separate day. Keep it separate from a central-city market circuit if you would rather spend your time eating and exploring than transferring between areas.

For friends, agree on a shared spending range and a meeting point. For a couple, choose one meal each person cares about and leave the rest open. Solo travelers can plan around the places and ordering arrangements they feel comfortable with.

## Before ordering or booking

- Ask about ingredients directly; a translated dish name is not a complete ingredient list.
- Confirm portion sizes and whether a minimum order applies.
- Keep a second meal choice for different tastes or a closed business.
- For a paid class or tour, check the language, meeting point, inclusions and cancellation terms.
