*Editorial guide · Updated November 15, 2026*

April is usually the month when spring finally feels settled in Seoul. The blossoms come into their own, the daylight stretches well past dinner, and the parks fill with people who waited all winter to sit outside. That makes April the most rewarding month and the most crowded one. This page covers how to get the good parts of the season without queueing for them.

## What the weather actually means for your days

April is the first month many visitors can walk a full district without a coat, and the days are long enough for an evening market to still be busy when you get there. Conditions are usually mild and dry, with rain that arrives as occasional showers rather than long spells. April is therefore the month to spend your hours outside and to stop treating the museum as the default answer.

Two things still need attention: the wind, which is common across Korea in spring and can be sharp on an exposed riverbank, and afternoons that feel warm in the sun and cool in the shade. A layer on your back still matters, particularly for children and older relatives. The light is also at its most useful here, with clear low sun in the morning and long golden hours in the early evening.

## What to do in this month

Blossom crowds are the defining feature, and they are concentrated rather than general. A handful of parks and riverbanks become extremely busy at the same hours, while most of the city continues as normal. The useful move is to pick one famous spot for an early weekday morning, get there before the crowds build, and then spend the rest of the day somewhere less contested.

The Han River becomes genuinely pleasant in April. The riverside paths, the public parks and the waterfront neighbourhoods are better in spring than in any other season, and you can walk them without a plan. Our [starter outline](/seoul-starter/) suggests a day that fits this shape, and the [food neighbourhood guide](/seoul-food-neighborhood-plan/) is worth following because outdoor markets reopen properly by April.

Do one longer day outside the city if you can. Blossom usually arrives later and lasts longer in the mountains and rural areas around Seoul than in the centre, and the same weekend that is packed in the city can be empty a couple of hours away. Check the regional timing through [VISITKOREA](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr) first.

## What to wear and carry

Light layers are the answer. A shirt or thin top, a sweatshirt or light jumper, and a jacket you can carry rather than wear cover most of April. Add a compact umbrella, because the showers are frequent even when the day is not wet from end to end.

Comfortable shoes are the single most useful thing you can pack this month. April walking days are long and the distances between the good spots are longer than they look on a map. A hat and sunglasses are worth packing too, because the sun is stronger than the temperature suggests once the wind drops.

## Days to plan around

Weekends are the busiest days of the month, and the last weekend is usually the busiest of all, carrying both the blossom season and a full programme of events. Midweek mornings and late afternoons are the calmest windows. Our [weekly listings](/seoul-weekly/) show the dated programme so you can check what is running before you fix a day.

April is also the start of the school and university holiday stretch in Korea, so family groups are on the move through the month. If you are travelling with children, the [family trip plan](/seoul-family-trip-plan/) keeps the day short, with two core stops and a meal pause, which is the version that usually works.

Finally, confirm that anything you plan to book has a current opening calendar rather than assuming an attraction runs every day of the month. Check the venue directly, and use the [before-you-book checklist](/seoul-before-you-book-checklist/) to keep track of what still needs confirming.
