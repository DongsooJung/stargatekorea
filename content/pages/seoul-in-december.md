*Editorial guide · Updated November 15, 2026*

December is cold, dry and unusually quiet in a way that surprises first-time visitors. The year-end work period winds down, small businesses close for the year at different points, and the streets fill with lights instead of people. It is a good month for museums, palaces in low winter light, and long meals indoors. It is a less easy month if you rely on a fixed restaurant booking or a shop that closes when you happen to arrive.

## What the weather actually means for your days

December is dry and cold, with the same sharp, clear quality that November has but more of it. Snow is possible in the city but is not the normal expectation, and the main thing to plan around is the cold itself, which settles in properly and stays. It is the month where the difference between a warm coat and a thin one decides whether you enjoy the outdoors or hide from it.

Daylight is at its shortest of the year, so the usable outdoor hours sit between mid-morning and mid-afternoon. The advantage is that this is also the best light of the year for the palaces and older streets, and the evenings are long enough to spend indoors without it feeling like an early night. Dry air returns in December, so keep something for your hands and lips.

## What to do in this month

Illuminations are the headline, and they run through the season in several districts. They are free to look at, but they are also the reason certain streets and parks are busy at specific hours, so plan them as an evening stop rather than a surprise. The [design neighbourhood guide](/seoul-design-neighborhood-plan/) helps you combine a lit-up walk with the shops and cafes that stay open around it.

Palaces in December are worth the early start. The crowds are thinner than in spring, the light is low and clear, and the courtyards look their best in the first hours after opening. The [culture-at-your-own-pace guide](/seoul-culture-at-your-pace/) gives you the shape of a Jongno day, and the [weather-flexible plan](/seoul-rain-and-weather-plan/) covers the cold-weather version of a backup day.

Food is the safest December plan. Korean cooking suits cold weather better than almost anything else, and the year-end season brings more restaurants running late. The [food neighbourhood plan](/seoul-food-neighborhood-plan/) is built around markets and neighbourhoods rather than reservations, which is the right approach this month.

## What to wear and carry

The full winter list: a warm coat that blocks wind, layers underneath, gloves, a hat and a scarf. This is the month to bring a second pair of gloves if you lose things easily, because cold hands end an evening early.

Footwear matters. Choose something insulated with a good grip for cold, possibly icy paving, and long days on stone. A thin waterproof shell is worth carrying, since a wet winter day is colder than a dry one. Sunglasses are still useful for the low winter sun on a clear day.

Pack for dryness: lip balm, hand cream, and a small humidifier if your accommodation allows one. A spare set of gloves and socks is the most useful backup to carry.

## Days to plan around

December has the most uneven calendar of the year. Many small shops and restaurants close for the year-end break at different points, sometimes for only a day and sometimes for several. There is no single fixed rule, so check the opening information of the specific places you want to visit rather than assuming, and keep a second option nearby for every stop.

Late December is the quietest and busiest at the same time: offices wind down, but the holiday itself pulls people into the city. Weekday afternoons after the work rush are often the calmest period of the whole month. Our [weekly listings](/seoul-weekly/) show the dated programme.

New Year and the Lunar New Year season fall close together in the calendar, and Seollal usually lands in late January or February. Check the official public holiday calendar on [Korea.kr](https://www.korea.kr) and the [Visit Korea holiday pages](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr) so you know when the Lunar New Year season usually starts to affect shop hours and closures in the city. The [before-you-book checklist](/seoul-before-you-book-checklist/) is worth reading before any non-refundable purchase.
