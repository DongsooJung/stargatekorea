February is the coldest-feeling month of the Seoul year, and the difference between a good day and a bad one comes down to your coat and your timing. The light stays low, the air stays dry, and the streets thin out once the holiday period passes. This page covers what the month is really like.

## What the weather actually means for your days

The pattern in February is cold, dry and mostly bright. Very little usually falls, so the outdoor part of the day is limited by how long you can stay comfortable rather than by rain. Plan the best part of your day for the morning and the worst for the middle of the afternoon.

Cold also changes what a neighbourhood feels like. In summer people spread out into the parks; in February they pull inward into covered arcades, tea rooms and small restaurants, and going inside every thirty minutes is normal.

## What to do in this month

February is the month for going up. The ski season around Seoul usually runs through it, and resorts in the mountains north of the city and further out in Gangwon Province are within reach of a day trip. Check conditions, lift and lesson arrangements and what hire includes, and book transport before the day itself.

If you stay in the city instead, spend one day in Jongno: Gyeongbokgung, the lanes of Bukchon and a tea stop fill a morning without feeling rushed, and the [culture-at-your-own-pace guide](/seoul-culture-at-your-pace/) keeps it to two anchors.

Make another day about food rather than sights. The [food neighbourhood plan](/seoul-food-neighborhood-plan/) works well here because a warm meal is the anchor rather than the reward.

## What to wear and carry

February is the month to bring the serious cold-weather layer. A proper winter coat, thermal layers underneath, gloves, a hat that covers your ears and a scarf are not optional extras for a full day outdoors. Hand warmers in a pocket are more useful than they sound if you plan to stand in a queue.

On your feet, choose insulated boots with grip on slick surfaces. Dry air is the other daily reality: a lip balm, hand cream and a room you can sleep in comfortably make the difference.

## Days to plan around

Seollal, the Lunar New Year, usually falls in January or February. If it lands in February, expect several consecutive days of reduced opening hours, sold-out trains and busy domestic travel corridors. The official public holiday calendar on [Korea.kr](https://www.korea.kr) and the [Visit Korea holiday pages](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr) will tell you where the holiday sits in a given year.

Outside the holiday, midweek is noticeably calmer than weekends, and the last weekend of a month tends to draw the busiest programme of events.
