*Editorial guide · Updated November 15, 2026*

January is one of Seoul's coldest and clearest months. Days are short, the air is dry, and the light is sharp, which suits anyone who would rather walk slowly than race between stops. This page covers what the month feels like, when Lunar New Year usually gets in the way, and what to carry.

## What the weather actually means for your days

Winter in Seoul is cold and dry rather than snowy, and rain is far less of a factor than in summer. An outdoor plan that falls apart usually falls apart because of the cold and the pace, not the forecast. Daylight is short, so a morning of sightseeing and an early evening meal make a comfortable pair, and the hours between them are better spent indoors.

Clear winter air also makes the hills look better than in hazy months, so a hill walk or a palace roof view is a good January bet. Check the forecast on the day and keep a [plan that can change with the weather](/seoul-rain-and-weather-plan/) at hand.

## What to do in this month

Start with a palace and put it first. Gyeongbokgung in the morning, before the courtyards fill, gives you space and low light for photographs. Hanbok rental around the palace usually runs seasonally, so check opening hours and group rules first. Add one slow stop afterwards — a tea house or a warm meal in Insadong — rather than a second landmark. Our [culture-first structure](/seoul-culture-at-your-pace/) is built for that kind of day.

Then take a separate trip east to Seongsu-dong, where the design shops and cafes are calmer in winter. Around the turn of the year, palaces commonly host a greeting ceremony where visitors bow to an official seated inside a gate.

## What to wear and carry

Think in layers and assume you will be outside longer than you expect. A warm coat over a mid-layer, with gloves, a hat and a scarf, is the difference between an enjoyable walk and a short one. Shoes should be comfortable with some grip, because polished floors and steps are common in older palaces.

Pack hand cream and a lip balm, and bring a charger and a small power bank for a day that starts outdoors and ends indoors.

## Days to plan around

Seollal, the Lunar New Year holiday, usually falls in late January or in February, depending on the lunar calendar. Travel between cities slows down, some shops and restaurants close for several days, and domestic travel demand rises sharply around it. Check the official public holiday calendar on [Korea.kr](https://www.korea.kr) and the [Visit Korea holiday information](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr) before booking trains or settling on a hotel area.

Beyond the holiday, weekends are busy in the shopping districts and calmer at the major sights, where the last weekend of a month usually carries the most events. Midweek mornings are the quietest time for a serious visit.
