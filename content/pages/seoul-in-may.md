*Editorial guide · Updated November 15, 2026*

May is the month most people would choose for Seoul if they could choose freely. The weather is usually comfortable, the parks are green, and the days are long enough to do something in the evening. It is also the month with the highest concentration of domestic family travel. This page covers what May feels like on the ground and whether the crowds are a problem for you.

## What the weather actually means for your days

May is the first reliably comfortable month. Days are usually warm enough for a light jacket and long enough to walk across the city without checking the clock, and the rain that does come tends to be short. Compared with March, the swing between morning and afternoon is smaller, which is what makes May feel easy.

The catch is that comfortable weather is shared. The same clear day that makes a riverside walk pleasant puts thousands of families on the same riverbank. Humidity is worth noticing too: the air is not yet heavy the way it becomes in July and August, but midday can feel warmer than the forecast label suggests.

## What to do in this month

The Han River is May's best asset. The waterfront neighbourhoods, the riverside paths and the public parks are all at their best, and many are free, open and easy to reach by subway. Add a market day and a district day and you have a complete trip without needing a single ticketed attraction.

For families, May is the strongest month of the year. The parks have playgrounds and open space, the museums have school holiday programmes, and the weather usually cooperates. Our [family trip plan](/seoul-family-trip-plan/) is built around two core stops and a meal pause, which is the version that survives a group with young children.

Universities are also worth a look in May. Campuses are usually in full term, the trees are out, and campus festivals and open events often run through the month. The [design neighbourhood guide](/seoul-design-neighborhood-plan/) points you towards the independent shops and small cafes that make these districts worth an afternoon.

If you eat well, spend the warm evening outdoors. The [food neighbourhood plan](/seoul-food-neighborhood-plan/) explains how to build a day around a market rather than around a list of restaurants.

## What to wear and carry

May is the first month where a light jacket and ordinary clothes cover almost every situation. Pack clothes you can walk in all day, a layer for the cool evening, and a hat and sunscreen rather than a heavy coat. A light layer is worth carrying for the air-conditioned interiors, which are genuinely cold.

Bring a compact umbrella for the occasional shower. If you are travelling with children, pack a change of clothes and a small snack: the day rarely turns into a problem, but everyone gets hungry earlier than you expect.

## Days to plan around

The long weekend at the start of May is the busiest stretch of the month, with domestic travel at its highest and accommodation in the city under the most pressure. If your dates are flexible, the second half of May is calmer.

Buddha's Birthday usually falls in May, and it is a lunar holiday, so its position in the calendar moves from year to year. Check the official public holiday information on [Korea.kr](https://www.korea.kr) and the [Visit Korea holiday pages](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr) before you book trains or plan a specific day; temples also publish their own visiting arrangements for the occasion.
