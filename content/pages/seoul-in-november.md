*Editorial guide · Updated November 15, 2026*

November is usually the month when everything about Seoul works at once. The weather is dry and clear, the heat is gone, the crowds of the autumn holidays have passed, and the ginkgo trees along the main streets turn a sharp yellow. It is also the month most visitors never consider, which is exactly why it can feel so calm. This page covers what November offers and how to plan around the quieter calendar.

## What the weather actually means for your days

November's character is crisp and dry. Rain is uncommon, the humidity is low, and the air is often clearer than it has been since spring. Once the first real cold settles, the skies are usually the kind of deep, flat blue that makes a city look well lit, and the low sun in the afternoons gives the streets a warm quality that photographs beautifully.

The trade-off is a definite drop in temperature, and it comes sooner than some visitors expect. The pattern is a cold morning and a cold evening, with a clear, bright middle of the day. That is a good pattern to plan around: it makes walking early and late uncomfortable and the middle of the day the best part, which is the opposite of July.

November is also when the year's end starts to appear in the city. The Lunar New Year season usually begins to reach the shops and markets later in the month, with the ingredients, wrapping paper and small gifts that Korea uses for Seollal appearing first in the big markets and department stores. Seollal itself usually falls in late January or February, so check the official public holiday calendar on [Korea.kr](https://www.korea.kr) and the [Visit Korea holiday pages](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr) for the year you travel.

## What to do in this month

The museums are the strongest reason to come in November. Autumn is when new programmes and exhibitions usually open, and the galleries are noticeably less full than in the blossom and festival months. The [culture-at-your-own-pace guide](/seoul-culture-at-your-pace/) is the right structure for this: one palace, one museum, and the time to actually look at both.

The parks are the second reason. Ginkgo and late maple colour line the avenues in the large city parks and along the river, and because the visitors are fewer, you can walk them properly rather than standing in a queue for a photograph. The riverside neighbourhoods are pleasant in November too, and much less crowded than in April or October.

A third possibility is a longer day out of the city, where the winter season in the smaller towns is quieter and the food is at its most comforting. ## What to wear and carry

November needs a real coat. A warm, windproof outer layer over a jumper, with a hat and gloves for the early morning and the evening, covers almost everything the city asks of you. A scarf is the item that makes the biggest difference to how the cold feels.

Below the coat, layers are better than one thick garment, because you will be moving between heated interiors and cold streets. Shoes should have grip for the leaves and for early-morning surfaces. Sunglasses remain useful because the light is clear and low. Bring a lip balm and hand cream: the air is dry again now that the summer humidity has gone.

## Days to plan around

November is a calm month, but the calendar still shapes it. The last weekend of a month usually carries the busiest programme of events, and the winter solstice period brings illuminations and seasonal programming into December. Our [weekly listings](/seoul-weekly/) show the dated programme so you can see how full the month is before you fix your days.

Weekdays are the easy part of November, and midweek mornings give you the palaces almost to yourself. Late afternoon is the best light, so aim for a walk then rather than at noon.

Because November is a shoulder season for accommodation, it is a good month to sort the practical details in advance. The [budget worksheet](/seoul-budget-worksheet/) is a useful place to set your own allowances before you book anything, and the [first hotel area guide](/where-to-stay-first-seoul-trip/) helps you pick a base that suits a quieter month of walking.
