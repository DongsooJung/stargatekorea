*Editorial guide · Updated November 15, 2026*

October is usually the month Seoul looks its best and works its hardest. The skies are often clearer than in any other month, the light is sharp, and the mountains around the city start to change colour towards the end of it. It is also the busiest month for visitors, and the weekends are noticeably harder than the weekdays. This page covers how to read the season correctly and keep the trip comfortable when everything is busy at once.

## What the weather actually means for your days

October is usually dry, crisp and unusually clear. This is the month when distant mountains become visible from the city on a normal day, and when a rooftop or a hill viewpoint genuinely rewards the climb. It is also when the difference between early and late October becomes obvious: the first half is usually mild and only lightly coloured, and the colour concentrates in the last third.

The dry weather is a gift for walking, and it is why October produces the best long outdoor days of the autumn. The trade-off is that clear autumn skies bring the biggest crowds, because domestic leaf-watching trips peak in exactly the same weeks. Heat is rarely an issue, but cold is not far off after sunset, so a layer you can put on is enough.

## What to do in this month

Get the mountains right. The hills north of the city and further out in Gyeonggi and Gangwon provinces usually change colour before Seoul's own streets do, and the popular trails become busy in the same weekends. If a full day out is on the table, weekday trails and the less popular routes are the difference between a good day and a queue at the car park. Check the regional timing reports on [VISITKOREA](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr).

In the city, October is the season for neighbourhoods. The design districts in Seongsu and the independent shops around Hongdae and Yeonnam are at their best when the weather is clear enough to spend an afternoon outside with a drink. The [design neighbourhood guide](/seoul-design-neighborhood-plan/) treats these as full days rather than stops between bigger attractions.

Food is easier in October than in the summer months, which is when the city eats outside more often. ## What to wear and carry

A light jacket, a layer underneath, and comfortable walking shoes are the core of an October packing list. Add a thin windproof layer for the hills and for the last weeks of the month, and a scarf for the clear evenings. Sunglasses are worth packing even though the air is cooler, because the low autumn sun is sharp.

Bring a camera or a phone with a spare battery, because the light quality in October is the best of the year and the day stretches past sunset. A small umbrella is still worth having for the occasional wet day.

## Days to plan around

October is the busiest month of the year in the city. Weekends at the popular parks, on the mountains and at the big events are genuinely busy, and the last weekend of a month is normally the busiest of the three. Weekday mornings and weekday afternoons are your best options for a palace, a museum or a trail.

Chuseok usually falls in September or October, and its position changes each year because it follows the lunar calendar. Confirm the date on the official public holiday calendar at [Korea.kr](https://www.korea.kr) and the [Visit Korea holiday pages](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr) before booking transport.
