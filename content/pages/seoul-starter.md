*Editorial guide · Updated November 12, 2026*

A good first Seoul trip leaves enough time to enjoy a neighborhood. Use these routes as a starting point, then move days around after checking opening calendars and the weather. They are suggestions for independent travel, not live directions or confirmed bookings.

**[Choose your trip length and pace](/#plan)** or [download the free arrival checklist](/downloads/seoul-first-trip-checklist.md).

## Before you choose activities

1. **Settle your airport-to-hotel journey.** Match your terminal and arrival time to the airport rail or other transport options, including the last leg to your hotel. See [AREX official information](https://www.arex.or.kr/main.do).
2. **Choose a hotel area.** Start with the neighborhood, transit access and luggage route. Use the [official Visit Seoul accommodation guide](https://english.visitseoul.net/Accommodation) and compare individual properties yourself.
3. **Arrange connectivity.** Check roaming, SIM and eSIM options against device compatibility and the data you need. [KT's visitor service](https://roaming.kt.com/eng/main) is one official source to compare.
4. **Leave arrival day light.** A long flight, immigration and hotel check-in can make a packed first day uncomfortable. Use the first evening for a short walk and a nearby meal.

## If you have one full day

### Old Seoul: Gyeongbokgung, Insadong and Ikseon-dong

Make the palace your anchor, then take time for tea, craft shops or a relaxed meal around Insadong. Add the lanes around Ikseon-dong only if you still feel like exploring. A slower version keeps just the palace and Insadong.

Check the palace's current calendar before setting the date. In poor weather, make a museum or indoor cultural stop the center of the day. A city-wide rush to tick off landmarks is optional; enjoying this one area can be enough.

## If you have three full days

Use the old Seoul day above, then add two contrasting days.

### Day 2: Market lunch, the stream and an evening in Myeongdong

Start with Gwangjang Market, walk a section of Cheonggyecheon if conditions suit and save the evening for Myeongdong. These are separate stops rather than one compulsory walking route: use transit when needed, and choose fewer stops if you prefer.

Check individual food stalls and venues before you go. Dietary requirements need to be confirmed with the business. If it rains, shorten the stream section and keep the day focused on indoor stops.

### Day 3: Hongdae and Yeonnam at a gentler pace

Make your way from the Hongik University Station area toward Yeonnam-dong and Gyeongui Line Forest Park. Leave room for a bookshop, independent shop or cafe rather than filling every hour. In poor weather, skip the park and keep the neighborhood stops indoors.

## If you have five full days

Add these to the three-day outline, with the order adjusted for opening calendars.

### Day 4: Seongsu and Seoul Forest

Group the day in eastern Seoul. Explore Seongsu-dong, choose a cafe or design stop and add Seoul Forest if the weather works. Individual shops, exhibitions and pop-ups have their own hours and may involve queues. Keep your plan flexible.

### Day 5: A museum day or a return to your favorite area

The National Museum of Korea is one possible anchor for a quieter final day. Confirm current museum and exhibition information directly. An equally good choice is to revisit the neighborhood you liked most, do some last shopping and leave time to pack.

## A few decisions that make the plan better

- **Choose your own walking load.** These routes have not been measured as accessible walking routes. Check station exits, slopes and step-free access for your needs using [Visit Seoul's accessible travel resources](https://english.visitseoul.net/).
- **Give weather a vote.** Switch a park or outdoor stretch for a museum, bookshop or cafe. Check venue access rather than assuming an indoor alternative is open.
- **Book only what you need.** Compare the date, meeting point, inclusions, language and cancellation terms before purchasing an activity. A ticket or pass is not automatically good value for your route.
- **Respect residential neighborhoods.** If adding Bukchon, follow posted visitor hours and local restrictions, keep noise down and avoid private entrances. See [the Korea Tourism Organization's Bukchon guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?menuSn=351&vcontsId=97932).
