Seoul has one of the easiest public transport systems in the world to use as a visitor, and almost all of it runs on a single tap-to-pay card: **T-money**. Here is everything you need to start using it on your first day.

## Where to buy it

You can buy a T-money card at:

- Convenience stores such as GS25, CU and 7-Eleven (look for the T-money logo at the counter)
- Ticket vending machines and service desks in subway stations
- Airport convenience stores, right after arrivals

The card itself costs a few thousand won and does not come preloaded, so add money as soon as you buy it. Many designs exist — including character cards — and they all work the same way.

## How to top it up

Top-ups are usually made in **cash**:

- At the counter of any convenience store that sells the card
- At the recharge machines inside every subway station (they have English menus)

Put the card on the reader, choose the amount, insert notes and wait until the screen confirms. Keep a bit of extra balance — if your balance is too low when you tap out, you will have to add money at the fare adjustment machine before you can leave the station.

## Tap in, tap out — on buses too

On the subway you tap at the gate when you enter and when you exit. On buses, tap the reader by the front door when you get on **and** tap the reader by the rear door when you get off. Forgetting to tap out on a bus is the most common mistake visitors make, because it means you lose the transfer discount.

## Free and discounted transfers

The fare is calculated by distance, and transfers between subway lines and buses are discounted or free when you:

- Tap out and tap in again within the transfer window (about 30 minutes in the daytime, longer late at night), and
- Have tapped out properly on the previous ride.

This is why tapping out on the bus matters — it starts the transfer clock.

## Taxis and beyond

Most taxis in Seoul accept T-money, which is handy when you have not yet set up a Korean payment card. The card also works on public transport in Busan, Daegu, Incheon and many other cities, and in convenience stores for small purchases.

## Quick tips

- **Do not keep two transit cards in the same wallet slot.** The gate may read the wrong one or refuse both.
- **Leftover balance can be refunded** at convenience stores before you leave Korea, minus a small fee.
- **Use a map app for routes.** Naver Map and Kakao Map show bus numbers, platform exits and real-time arrivals in English.
