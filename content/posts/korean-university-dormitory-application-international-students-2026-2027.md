<p><em>Editorial review draft — November 15, 2026. All application dates below are program-specific examples, not universal deadlines.</em></p>
<p>Applying for a dormitory in South Korea can be confusing for international students. Your housing application may be separate from university admission, and eligibility can vary between exchange programs, full-degree courses and Korean language institutes. This guide explains how to prepare, avoid common mistakes and verify the official application notice.</p>
<h2>1. Check which housing category applies to you</h2>
<p>Before comparing buildings, identify your student status and campus. Universities may assign different dormitories to exchange students, degree students, visiting researchers or language-program students. Yonsei University, for example, lists SK Global House and International House as options for international students, while access to Muak dormitories differs by program status. Its official housing information says policies may change each semester.</p>
<p>Find your department's teaching campus, not just the university's main administrative address. Some institutions operate separate campuses and dormitories with different application systems.</p>
<h2>2. Find the semester-specific official notice</h2>
<p>Search your university's international office and housing office websites for a notice matching the exact semester and program. Record the opening and closing time in Korea Standard Time (KST), eligibility, required documents, room fees, deposit or payment requirements, housing period, and refund conditions. Do not rely on an old blog post for a future application deadline.</p>
<p>As an example, Yonsei's May 15, 2026 notice for fall semester housing listed August 23 to December 21, 2026 as the residence period for exchange and visiting applicants. That is a <strong>past program-specific example</strong>, not a valid deadline for the next semester.</p>
<h2>3. Prepare your account and documents early</h2>
<p>Universities commonly use a student portal or dedicated dormitory application system. You might need an admission or student number before signing in. Check when the portal account becomes active; a delay on opening day can matter if rooms are allocated in application order.</p>
<p>Compile a personal checklist from the actual notice: admission details, passport identification, contact information, emergency contact, housing preferences and any required health documents. Do not assume a specific medical certificate or test is universally required. Requirements depend on the university, building and term, and should be checked through the official notice.</p>
<h2>4. Submit and verify the application</h2>
<p>Read every eligibility condition before selecting a building. Enter the information precisely and save a confirmation or screenshot with the application number and submission time. Check whether room allocation is first-come-first-served, lottery-based or priority-based. Being able to submit does not always mean a room is guaranteed.</p>
<p>If you want a roommate, confirm whether roommate requests are supported and whether both applicants need to submit matching information. Do not assume the request will be honored.</p>
<h2>5. Understand selection, payment and move-in</h2>
<p>After the application closes, universities may publish results through the portal rather than by email. Mark the announcement date and payment deadline separately. Some offers can lapse if the payment is not completed by the deadline.</p>
<p>Check accepted payment methods, transfer reference, refund rules, bedding provision, earliest check-in time and whether early arrival is allowed. Keep your receipt and confirmation. If your flight arrives before move-in, arrange alternative accommodation for those nights rather than assuming the dormitory can admit you early.</p>
<h2>6. Real official examples from 2026</h2>
<p>Yonsei University's June 2, 2026 Office of International Affairs notice directed fall 2026 applicants to its housing schedule and login instructions. Its different housing categories show why students need the correct program notice. Korea University's fall 2026 international undergraduate dormitory notice, meanwhile, specified an application window of June 17–21, 2026 and a separate July 1–12 results/payment period. These dates are historical examples, not current enrollment advice.</p>
<p>For another concrete example, Yonsei's 2026–2027 Winter Abroad notice listed different application and move-in dates for SK Global House, International House and Woo Jung Won. This confirms that even buildings within one university may have different arrangements.</p>
<h2>7. Have a backup housing plan</h2>
<p>Campus housing is limited. If your application is rejected or waitlisted, compare short-term accommodation with carefully verified private rental options. Review deposit terms, commuting routes and contract conditions before transferring money. For more detail, read our <a href="/student-housing-seoul-wolse-jeonse-rental-safety/">Student Housing in Seoul: Dorms, Wolse, Jeonse and Rental Safety</a> guide (internal link will become usable after publication).</p>
<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>Does university admission guarantee dormitory housing?</h3><p>No. Dormitory eligibility and allocation are separate and subject to availability and program-specific rules.</p>
<h3>Should exchange and degree students apply through the same form?</h3><p>Not necessarily. Universities can use different buildings and application procedures for each group.</p>
<h3>Can I pay after the dormitory payment deadline?</h3><p>Do not assume so. Check the official notice and contact the housing office immediately if you have a problem.</p>
