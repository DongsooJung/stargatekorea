If you plan to study, work toward admission or spend a long visit in South Korea, learning basic Korean can make everyday activities much easier. Even when university classes are taught in English, you may need Korean at a restaurant, in a pharmacy or while asking for directions.

You do not have to buy a private course to get started. Several public institutions offer online learning resources. This guide explains how to find the official options and organize your first four weeks.

## Start with the Online King Sejong Institute

The [Online King Sejong Institute](https://www.iksi.or.kr/lms/main/about.do) describes itself as a **free online Korean language learning service** available on computers and mobile devices.

Its structured materials can help beginners study Korean writing, pronunciation, basic vocabulary and common expressions. Courses exist for different proficiency levels, and the online school provides more than one learning format.

If you are new to Korean, start with an introductory course instead of immediately attempting advanced grammar or exam preparation.

A useful first goal is to recognize **Hangul**, Korea's writing system, and read everyday words. This foundation supports later listening and speaking practice.

## Choose a suitable learning format

Online Korean language platforms can offer self-paced learning and instructor-supported classes. These are not identical experiences.

A self-paced course is flexible for travelers or students who cannot join lessons at set times. An instructor-supported course may offer feedback or scheduled activities.

Always look at the specific course page. Registration dates, languages of instruction, availability and participation requirements can differ. A course catalog is not a guarantee that every class is currently accepting applications.

For example, the Online King Sejong Institute posted an **introductory course cancellation and change notice effective November 16, 2026**. Check the current schedule before registering rather than relying on a previous semester's dates.

## Add free practice from official resources

The [King Sejong Institute's online learning materials](https://www.sejonghakdang.org/home/) include resources for vocabulary, grammar, conversation and Korean culture.

Try combining a short lesson with listening or pronunciation practice. This is more manageable than collecting multiple courses without completing them.

You can focus on familiar situations: ordering a meal, shopping, asking the time, using public transport and introducing yourself politely.

Save a small set of new expressions each day and review them before starting the next lesson.

## What about TOPIK?

The **Test of Proficiency in Korean (TOPIK)** is relevant to many learners, but examination and admission requirements depend on the university and program.

The Korean government's [Study in Korea Korean-language guide](https://studyinkorea.go.kr/en/plan/examAndKoreanStudy.do) identifies official learning resources, including free beginner-level TOPIK I lectures in multiple languages.

Use the resources for practice without assuming they replace a recognized examination result. If you need a certificate for admission or another administrative purpose, verify the precise requirement with the responsible institution.

Do not assume all international students need the same TOPIK level. English-taught and Korean-taught programs can have different admissions policies.

## A four-week beginner study plan

This is an illustrative schedule rather than an official curriculum or a promise of proficiency.

**Week 1 — Hangul and sounds.** Learn how the alphabet is arranged into syllables. Practice reading short words and writing simple names.

**Week 2 — Greetings and introductions.** Learn common polite expressions and how to say where you are from, what you study and what you need.

**Week 3 — Everyday tasks.** Practice restaurant conversations, numbers, directions and transportation vocabulary. Read short signs you might encounter during a normal day.

**Week 4 — Listening and review.** Repeat short recordings, record yourself speaking and revisit difficult phrases. Review vocabulary instead of introducing too many new topics at once.

A daily session of 20–30 minutes may be a practical starting point for some learners, but change the schedule to fit your circumstances.

## Practice after arriving in Korea

Real life provides useful opportunities to reinforce your lessons. Try reading signs at subway stations or speaking briefly when ordering food.

You can also look for language exchange events organized by your university or local community, if available. Confirm current schedules and eligibility before joining.

Translation apps remain useful. For serious legal, medical or rental matters, use qualified interpretation rather than relying on beginner Korean.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the Online King Sejong Institute free?**  
Its official introduction describes a free online Korean-learning service. Check individual course schedules and enrollment conditions.

**Can I practice for TOPIK without a paid tutor?**  
Yes. Government learning portals provide free practice and study materials, but official exams may have separate registration and fees.

**Does every international student need TOPIK?**  
No universal requirement should be assumed. Confirm your university and specific program's published admission rules.
