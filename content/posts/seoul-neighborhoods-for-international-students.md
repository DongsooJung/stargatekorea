Where you live in Seoul shapes your daily life more than almost anything else: your commute, your weekend plans, even how quickly you make friends. Most international students end up in a handful of university neighborhoods. Here is a quick tour.

## Sinchon and Ewha — the classic student district

**Nearby universities:** Yonsei, Ewha Womans, Sogang, Hongik is one stop away

Sinchon is the busiest student area in the city: cafés open late, cheap restaurants, study rooms on every block and a lot of English spoken around campus. It is lively and central, with direct subway access on Line 2 and buses everywhere. The trade-off is noise at night and higher demand for rooms near the start of each semester.

## Hongdae — for nightlife and the arts

**Nearby universities:** Hongik, and an easy ride to Sinchon schools

Hongdae is famous for street performances, live music, independent shops and nightlife. It is a fun place to live if you like being in the middle of things, and well connected by Line 2, the Gyeongui-Jungang Line and the airport railroad. Quieter streets toward Mangwon and Yeonnam are popular with students who want the atmosphere without the weekend crowds.

## Anam — compact and campus-centered

**Nearby university:** Korea University

Anam is smaller and more focused around one campus. Rents are often more moderate than in Sinchon, and daily life revolves around the university. Line 6 connects you to the rest of the city.

## Hoegi and Imun — a quieter east side

**Nearby universities:** Kyung Hee, Hankuk University of Foreign Studies (HUFS)

This area has a strong international-student community thanks to HUFS and Kyung Hee. It is calmer and more residential, with good-value food and housing. Hoegi Station sits on Line 1 and the Gyeongui-Jungang Line.

## Hyehwa — university town with a theater scene

**Nearby university:** Sungkyunkwan (Humanities and Social Sciences campus)

Around Hyehwa you will find Daehangno, Seoul's theater district, plus parks and historic streets near the palaces. It feels more cultural and a little more grown-up than the bigger student hubs. Line 4 runs through it.

## Seoul National University area — Nokdu and Seoul Nat'l Univ. Station

**Nearby university:** Seoul National University

SNU's campus sits in the hills of Gwanak. Students tend to live around Seoul Nat'l Univ. Station and the Nokdu area, where rents are known to be some of the most affordable among the major university districts. Expect a shuttle bus or a short bus ride to campus; Line 2 and the Sillim Line connect the area to the city.

## What kind of housing to expect

- **Dormitories:** The easiest option for your first semester. Apply early — places are limited.
- **One-room (원룸):** A studio apartment, usually rented with a deposit plus monthly rent (*wolse*). Deposits vary widely, so compare listings carefully.
- **Goshiwon / goshitel:** Very small furnished rooms with shared facilities and no large deposit. Good as a short-term landing spot while you look for something better.
- **Share houses:** Furnished rooms in shared apartments, often marketed to foreigners, with simpler contracts.

Before you sign anything, visit the room in person, check the contract terms carefully and ask your university's international office if anything is unclear.
