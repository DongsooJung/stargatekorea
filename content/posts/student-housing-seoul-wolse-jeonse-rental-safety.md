<p>Finding student accommodation in Seoul requires more than choosing an attractive neighborhood. International students need to compare upfront deposits, monthly expenses, commute times, building conditions and contract terms. Start with your university's housing office, then compare verified private options.</p>
<h2>Start with university housing</h2>
<p>Campus dormitories can reduce commuting and simplify the move to Korea. Ask about eligibility for your academic program, application dates, room assignment rules, meal plans and refund terms. A university admission offer does not guarantee a dormitory place. Degree students, exchange students and language students may have different application routes.</p>
<h2>Understand wolse and jeonse</h2>
<p><strong>Wolse</strong> generally involves an upfront deposit plus monthly rent. Compare the total initial payment, rent, management charges, utilities and any brokerage fee. <strong>Jeonse</strong> generally involves a substantial deposit and can create significant deposit-recovery risk. Neither format is automatically right for every student.</p>
<h2>Compare total costs, not only rent</h2>
<p>Make a simple spreadsheet with the deposit, recurring housing expenses, expected commute time, internet and heating charges, and lease duration. Pay attention to the building's actual location relative to your department, not just the university's main gate. A lower advertised rent can be offset by extra commuting or management charges.</p>
<h2>Inspect the room and landlord information</h2>
<p>Check ventilation, plumbing, heating, locks, windows, appliances, photographs of existing damage, and included furnishings. Confirm who is authorized to rent the unit and review the registered ownership and claims with qualified local assistance where necessary. Do not transfer large sums merely because an online listing says there is a limited-time offer.</p>
<h2>Read the contract carefully</h2>
<p>Confirm the precise address, lease dates, deposit amount, payment schedule, management fees, repair responsibilities and return conditions. Keep copies of the signed contract, transfers and messages. Korean procedures such as lease reporting or a fixed-date confirmation can have specific applicability and effects; seek official guidance rather than assuming a signature by itself protects every deposit.</p>
<h2>Get support in a language you understand</h2>
<p>The Seoul Foreign Portal and Seoul Foreign Resident Center offer resources for international residents, including rental guidance. Availability, languages and opening hours can change, so confirm before arranging a visit. You can also ask your university's international student office for an explanation of housing policies and suitable local support.</p>
<h2>Before you commit</h2>
<ul><li>Compare at least two realistic locations and commuting routes.</li><li>Request a full breakdown of deposits, rent and additional charges.</li><li>Verify the landlord or representative and the unit's identity.</li><li>Read a reliable translation of every contract clause.</li><li>Keep a record of the room's condition and your payment receipts.</li><li>Have a temporary accommodation alternative if the property cannot be verified.</li></ul>
<h2>Questions to ask before paying</h2><p>Ask whether the management fee is fixed or varies with heating and other utilities, whether bills are paid directly to providers or through the landlord, and how the room will be handed over at the end of the contract. Request written clarification about cleaning fees, furniture and repairs. A rental advertisement does not necessarily describe every cost of living in the property.</p><p>For a student entering Korea for the first time, the timing of an apartment contract matters as much as its price. Make sure you have a realistic plan for your arrival date, temporary stay and any period between moving out of one place and moving into another. If the contract is in Korean and you do not understand a condition, obtain independent interpretation before signing. Keep screenshots of the listing and the final contract so you can compare what was promised with what you agreed to.</p><h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>Is a dormitory always cheaper?</h3><p>No. Compare the full semester price, room type, meal plan and alternatives. Costs vary by institution.</p>
<h3>Can international students rent private accommodation?</h3><p>Many do, but the agreement and individual administrative requirements need careful checking.</p>
<h3>What if a contract looks suspicious?</h3><p>Do not transfer a substantial deposit until you have obtained independent verification or qualified advice.</p>
